## 📖 The Story

### The Client

Savannah Gates Hotel is a 90-room hotel in Gaborone, Botswana. They have four room types:
- 40 Standard Rooms (business travelers)
- 25 Deluxe Rooms
- 10 Executive Suites (high-end corporate clients)
- 15 Family Rooms (tourists and holiday guests)

The hotel operates year-round with busy periods during safari season (June-September), major holidays, and local business events. Annual revenue is approximately P31 million.

---

### The Problems They Hired Me to Solve

When I came in as a Data Analyst, the hotel was facing three major issues that were costing them money every single day:

#### Problem 1: Revenue Leakage from Static Pricing
The hotel charged the exact same room rates every single day — whether they were 10% full or 90% full, whether there was a major conference in town or a quiet Tuesday. During high-demand periods, rooms sold out quickly at prices that were far too low. During slow periods, rooms sat empty because rates were too high to attract guests.

**What this cost them:** An estimated P3.7 million in lost revenue annually.

#### Problem 2: Guest Feedback Was Scattered and Ignored
Guest reviews came in daily from four different platforms — Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Google Reviews, and Expedia. Negative reviews about slow WiFi, noise complaints, or cleanliness issues sat unnoticed for weeks. By the time management responded, the guest was long gone and the bad review had already influenced other potential guests.

**What this cost them:** A stagnant average rating of 3.88/5.0 and a damaged reputation that could have been easily fixed.

#### Problem 3: Staffing Was Based on Guesswork
The same number of staff were scheduled every day regardless of how many guests were actually in the hotel. On quiet Tuesdays with 40% occupancy, the hotel was overstaffed — paying housekeepers, front desk staff, and restaurant workers with nothing to do. On busy weekends with 90% occupancy, departments were understaffed and service quality suffered.

**What this cost them:** P77,000 wasted annually on unnecessary shifts.

---

### What I Discovered in Their Data

I analyzed a full year of hotel operations data and found patterns nobody had noticed:

- **Event days generate 35% more revenue** than normal days — but room prices never changed during events
- **Weekend occupancy is 15% higher** than weekdays — yet weekend rates were identical to Tuesday rates
- **71.6% of guest reviews are positive** — but the 15.3% negative reviews were never tracked or addressed quickly
- **496 shifts across the year were overstaffed** — mostly housekeeping on quiet weekday afternoons
- **WiFi quality was the #1 guest complaint** — mentioned in 23 negative reviews, but management had no idea because nobody was tracking this

The data was screaming for change. Nobody was listening.

---

### The Solutions I Built

I didn't just hand over a report with charts. I built two working tools the hotel can actually use every day.

#### Solution 1: Management Dashboard
**An interactive app that puts all hotel data in one place.**

Instead of checking four different websites for reviews and digging through spreadsheets for revenue numbers, the manager now opens one dashboard and sees everything:

- **Revenue Overview** — How much money came in this month, how it compares to previous months, which days are busiest, what the average room rate is
- **Guest Satisfaction** — All reviews from all platforms in one view, color-coded by sentiment (green = happy, red = unhappy), average ratings, top complaints
- **Staffing Efficiency** — Which departments are overstaffed, how much money is being wasted, when the hotel needs more or fewer staff
- **Pricing Strategy** — How events impact revenue, the relationship between occupancy and room rates

**Real impact:** Managers now see problems the same day they happen instead of weeks later.

#### Solution 2: Hotel Operations System
**A working tool the front desk uses daily to make better decisions.**

This system has three practical features:

**Dynamic Pricing Calculator:**
The front desk clerk enters a check-in date, selects a room type, and inputs current occupancy. The system automatically checks if there's a local event, if it's a weekend, and calculates the optimal room rate based on demand.

*Example:* During the Mining Conference weekend at 90% occupancy, an Executive Suite that normally costs P2,100 should be priced at P3,130. That's 49% more revenue per room — just by charging what the room is actually worth.

**Centralized Review Hub:**
All guest reviews from all platforms appear in one feed, sorted by most recent. Red-bordered cards for negative reviews catch management's attention immediately. WiFi complaints are now spotted and fixed the same day.

**Daily Status Snapshot:**
A quick view showing today's occupied rooms, today's revenue, and current staff on duty — everything a manager needs for the morning briefing.
